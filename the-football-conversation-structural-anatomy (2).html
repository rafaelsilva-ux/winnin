00Executive summary

What two seasons of signal say

  • The Premier League is about 10% of the global English-language soccer conversation. A brand entering it enters a sub-territory, not the category.
  • The highest-engagement niches are off-pitch — player lives, skills, transfers. The match is not the top of the conversation.
  • Volume follows a ritual calendar: season open, festive fixtures, transfer windows, title run-in. None of it requires a result.
  • Anticipation outperforms reaction. Pre-match atmosphere content beats post-match coverage at every level of the dataset.
  • Formats reward reflection over coverage — parody, satisfying, Q&A and ASMR all rank above live and news.
  • Two communities outperform their size and carry no brand presence: the UK fan creator layer and the South Asian diaspora.

04Who Is Making the Conversation

Five distinct communities, different purposes

The PL creator ecosystem is not one community. It is five, each producing different content with different motivations — and only one of them is producing content that could be described as "owned by the audience."

1

The rights holders — clubs, league & broadcast

Premier League, Sky Sports, Man City, Liverpool, Arsenal, Chelsea, Man United

The PL official account appears in three of the top ten most-engaged videos in the dataset — but it is producing short creator-style clips (Bernardo Silva reaction, Giroud scorpion kick throwback, Amad Diallo composure), not match coverage. The rights holder has learned to operate like a creator, not a broadcaster. Sky Sports sits in the brand cluster but not in the top-engagement cluster.

@premierleague TikTok (27.3M) @premierleague Instagram (80.3M) @mancity TikTok (37.7M) @liverpoolfc TikTok (30M) @skysports Instagram
2

The global creator class — aggregators & packagers

Overtime, 433, BR Football, Goal Global, Sky Sports PL YouTube

Primarily US-based or globally distributed. They produce ranked lists, compilations, viral-moment packages, and "top 5" formats. Platform-fluent but not culturally rooted in English football. This is where the competition-format content lives — the PL packaged for a global audience, stripped of terrace context.

@overtime TikTok (35.3M) @433 Instagram (79.7M) @brfootball TikTok @goalglobal TikTok
3

The UK fan creator layer — terrace voice & British humour

lore.ofthe.terraces, NayeemUTDFC, 442oons, danielutd1, fan channels

Smaller subscriber counts (1–5M), high cultural specificity, terrace language, British understatement. The second most-engaged video in the entire two-season dataset is a fan creator — @lore.ofthe.terraces — explaining British football culture to an American audience. 4.1M engagement. No institutional backing. This is where authentic fan voice lives, and it is structurally unclaimed by brands.

@lore.ofthe.terraces — 4.1M engagement @442oons YouTube (4.7M subs) @NayeemUTDFC TikTok (1.3M) @danielutd1 TikTok (806K)
4

The South Asian diaspora — a distinct & growing sub-community

Star Sports India, Being Suku, Ranveer Allahbadia, PL India fan reels

A structurally distinct community producing PL content for Indian audiences. Star Sports India (11M+ subscribers) appears in the brand cluster. The "Premier League India Fan Reels" niche — only 331 posts — carries an average engagement of 29,239, suggesting a tight, highly engaged community that outperforms its size. The PL's biggest growth audience is already inside this conversation, and largely unclaimed by any Western consumer brand.

@starsportsindia Instagram (11M) Being Suku YouTube (6.9M) Ranveer Allahbadia YouTube (11.3M) PL India Reels: 29K avg engagement
5

The entertainment crossover — football as vehicle

MrBeast, IShowSpeed, Shakira, LEGO, celebrity one-offs

Content that uses football as a vehicle for entertainment rather than football for its own sake. MrBeast's "Confronting Ronaldo" pulled 13.9M engagement and 304M views — dwarfing any match moment in the dataset. But this is celebrity conversation that happens to involve a footballer. It represents attention volume, not football culture. Reaching it requires different logic than partnering with a club.

MrBeast: 304M views per football video IShowSpeed Instagram LEGO #FIFAWorldCup: 17.9M engagement

Only one of the five communities produces content the audience treats as its own — and it is the one layer no brand currently occupies.

The ASMR signal

ASMR reaching the top 5 formats at Premier League level is unexpected and specific. It maps to content that offers slow access to the game — stadium sounds, training ground atmosphere, terrace noise. There is a community that wants presence at the game, not highlights from it. Stadium audio, ambient matchday content, and real-time sensory access are the format expression of this. It is also format-territory that no rights holder or brand sponsor currently occupies at scale.

06Language & Tone

Club identity, not match events. Wit, not hype.

What the Tags Reveal

Club = identity, not competition

The taxonomy is club names — not "goals", "matchday", or "reactions". The PL conversation organises itself around belonging to a club, not around watching a competition.

#fyp and #soccer = US audience targeting

#soccer in a UK football conversation, and #fyp (For You Page), are algorithmic reach tags — creators deliberately targeting US audiences. The English-language football conversation is partly a US-audience product.

Tone Patterns in Top-Engaged Content

Wit and understatement

"Cancel the appointment" (a player's haircut styled like a cult hero). "Cold Palmer." "So satisfying…" The emotional register is dry, not triumphant. British football humour is a signal of belonging — outsiders don't get it.

Aesthetic reverence

"Breathtaking." "Special." "Composed amid the madness." Top content responds to how something looked — not whether it led to a win. The aesthetic of the moment outweighs its result.

Belonging signals

"#britishhumour", "#terraces", "#banter". Used most intensely when explaining UK football to non-UK audiences. The conversation constantly re-establishes what it is and what it feels like to be part of it.

The central structural finding

The conversation is ritual-heavy and result-resistant. The moments that consistently generate the most volume and engagement are not produced by what happened on Saturday. They are produced by transfer windows, player personalities, season cadences, cultural identity content, and the aesthetics of how the game looks and feels — not what it delivered. The match moment can break through, but it competes against a backdrop of pre-scheduled, evergreen, and personality-driven content that does not depend on a result.

09Strategic Recommendations

Where to stand, and why

Six recommendations, each traceable to a specific structural finding. Ordered by time horizon — immediate to long-term.

Immediate

1. Build a ritual calendar, not a match calendar

The brand's activation calendar should be structured around the six predictable volume surges — season open (August), transfer deadline days (January & September), festive fixtures (December), UCL knockout rounds (February–April), title run-in (April), and season end. These are guaranteed attention peaks that require no favourable result to materialise.

Evidence: The volume structure across 22 months shows that every major traffic spike maps to a calendar ritual, not to a match result. May 2026 (93K posts) is the dataset peak — and it was fully predictable before the season began.
Immediate

2. Own the moment before the whistle — not after

Pre-match and anticipation content consistently outperforms post-match reaction content. Man City's "Final preparations" (3.35M engagement, 66.6M views) is pre-match atmosphere. The season-open surge dwarfs individual matchweek spikes. The audience's peak emotional state is before the outcome is known, not after — and that window is sponsor-plannable, not hostage to results.

Evidence: "Final preparations" (66.6M views) outperformed the Marmoush hat-trick (39.7M views) — a genuinely extraordinary match moment — purely on anticipation vs. reaction framing. The format data confirms it: Satisfying > Live > News at the PL level.
Short term — this season

3. Partner with the UK fan creator layer — before it gets claimed

The second most-engaged video in the entire dataset is a fan creator with no institutional backing — @lore.ofthe.terraces, 4.1M engagement. This community (442oons, NayeemUTDFC, danielutd1, terrace-language creators) produces the most culturally specific, audience-trusted content in the PL conversation. No brand sponsor currently inhabits this layer. The window to enter it as a first mover is open.

Evidence: @lore.ofthe.terraces: 4.1M engagement, 32.3M views, no brand partnership visible. Fan creator cluster sits entirely outside the brand-cluster data — no consumer brand appears alongside these creators.
Short term — this season

4. Activate the South Asian diaspora community directly

The Premier League India fan niche — 331 posts, 29K average engagement — is performing at 3x the average engagement efficiency of general PL content. Star Sports India (11M subscribers) already sits in the PL brand cluster. This is a tight, highly engaged community that is culturally distinct from the mainstream UK fan conversation and largely unaddressed by UK-headquartered sponsors. The point of entry is diaspora-specific storytelling, not translated UK content.

Evidence: PL India fan reels: 29,239 avg engagement at only 331 posts — outlier efficiency. 4 India-origin creators in the PL trending creator cluster. Star Sports India in the brand cluster.
Longer term

5. Build in the ASMR and sensory access format

ASMR reaching the top 5 formats at PL level represents an underserved community that wants presence at the game, not highlights from it — stadium sounds, training ground atmosphere, matchday rituals. This is format-territory that no rights holder occupies at scale, and it maps directly to a drinks brand's natural sensory territory (cold can, crowd noise, anticipation). A brand creating this content at live events owns a format the PL itself has not claimed.

Evidence: ASMR ranked 4th by avg engagement in the PL format data, above Challenge and News. No club or rights-holder creator appears as a leading ASMR producer in the creator clusters.
Longer term

6. Close season is not dead time — it's transfer culture

June–July produced 20–24K posts/month across both close seasons. The audience stays in the conversation year-round, driven by transfer rumour, kit launches, player life content, and prediction culture. A brand that treats the close season as "off" is structurally absent for 3 months while the audience is present. The opportunity is in the rituals of the gap: transfer deadline anticipation, squad reveal reactions, pre-season training access.

Evidence: Close season (June–July 2025): 44,000 avg posts/month. Secondary window (June–September 2026): 135K posts, 405M engagement — the conversation continues at full pace through no live football.
Structural constraint to note

The brand layer in the PL conversation is currently occupied by clubs, the league itself, Sky Sports, EA Sports FC, and Star Sports India — all institutional. No non-rights-holding consumer brand appears in the brand creator cluster at comparable scale. The rights structure means that match footage, goal highlights, and live broadcast content are walls, not doors. The strategic implication of this analysis is not to compete in the rights-held space — it is to establish presence in the ritual, personality, and cultural identity layer where rights do not apply and where, as the fan creator data shows, the engagement per post is higher anyway.
